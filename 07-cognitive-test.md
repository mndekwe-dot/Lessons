# 07 — Cognitive Assessment: Full Prep

The cognitive test measures reasoning **speed and accuracy**, not programming. Four sections:
**Numerical, Verbal, Abstract, and General Mental Ability.** Most people don't finish — so
**accuracy on what you attempt beats rushing.** 30–60 seconds per question, guess-and-move-on
if stuck (guessing beats blank when there's no penalty — confirm on the day).

This guide teaches each section with **worked examples**, then gives you **practice questions
with answers** at the end. Do the practice under a timer.

---

# SECTION 1 — NUMERICAL REASONING

## 1a. Number sequences — find the rule

Check these rules **in this order** (first that fits wins):

| Rule | Looks like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Constant difference (+d or −d) | steady steps | 2, 5, 8, 11 → +3 → **14** |
| 2. Growing difference | steps get bigger | 1, 2, 4, 7, 11 → +1,+2,+3,+4 → **16** |
| 3. Multiply (×k) | numbers explode | 3, 6, 12, 24 → ×2 → **48** |
| 4. Alternating (two patterns) | zig-zag | 1, 10, 2, 20, 3 → odds +1, evens ×2 → **30** |
| 5. Squares / cubes / primes | familiar numbers | 1, 4, 9, 16 → squares → **25** |
| 6. Add the two before (Fibonacci) | each = sum of prev two | 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 → **13** |

**Worked example:** `4, 9, 16, 25, ?`
- Try constant difference: +5, +7, +9 → not constant, but the differences grow by 2 → could be rule 2.
- Try squares: 2²=4, 3²=9, 4²=16, 5²=25 → yes! Next is 6² = **36**.

**Worked example:** `2, 6, 18, 54, ?`
- Differences +4, +12, +36 — not constant. Numbers grow fast → try ×: 2×3=6, 6×3=18, 54×3 = **162**.

## 1b. Percentages — the fast tricks

Build any percentage from these three:
- **10%** = divide by 10 (move the decimal one left). 10% of 250 = 25
- **5%** = half of 10%. 5% of 250 = 12.5
- **1%** = divide by 100. 1% of 250 = 2.5

So **15% of 250** = 10% + 5% = 25 + 12.5 = **37.5**

**The swap trick — memorize this:** `x% of y = y% of x`
- 8% of 25 → flip → 25% of 8 = **2** (much easier!)
- 4% of 50 → 50% of 4 = **2**

**Increase / decrease:**
- Increase by 20% → ×1.20
- Decrease by 20% → ×0.80
- "What % is 30 of 120?" → 30/120 = 0.25 = **25%**

## 1c. Ratios

**Total = sum of the ratio parts.** Split the total into that many equal parts.

**Worked example:** share 40 sweets in ratio 3:2.
- Parts: 3 + 2 = 5
- One part = 40 ÷ 5 = 8
- So 3×8 = **24** and 2×8 = **16** (check: 24+16 = 40 ✓)

## 1d. Averages (mean)

**Average = total ÷ how many.**
- Average of 4, 8, 6 → (4+8+6) ÷ 3 = 18 ÷ 3 = **6**
- Reverse: "average of 3 numbers is 10, two are 8 and 9, find the third" → total must be 30; 30 − 8 − 9 = **13**

## 1e. Reading tables / charts

- Read the **question first**, then find only the numbers you need.
- Watch **units** (thousands? percentages? months?).
- "How much MORE did A make than B?" → subtract. "How many TIMES?" → divide.

# SECTION 2 — VERBAL REASONING (True / False / Cannot Say)

You get a short passage, then statements. Decide:

- **True** — the passage says it, or it clearly must follow from what's said.
- **False** — the passage says the opposite.
- **Cannot Say** — the passage doesn't give enough to decide.

## The one rule that matters

**Use ONLY the passage. Your own knowledge is a trap.** If it isn't stated or directly
implied, the answer is **Cannot Say** — even if you know it's true in real life.

## Worked example

> *Passage:* "The library opens at 9 am on weekdays. On Saturdays it opens at 10 am. It is
> closed on Sundays. Members may borrow up to 5 books at a time."

| Statement | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "The library opens at 9 am on Mondays." | **True** | Monday is a weekday → stated |
| "The library is open on Sundays." | **False** | passage says closed |
| "Non-members can borrow books." | **Cannot Say** | passage only mentions members — nothing about non-members |
| "The library has more than 1000 books." | **Cannot Say** | not mentioned at all (even if likely true!) |
| "On Saturday it opens later than on Tuesday." | **True** | 10 am vs 9 am → follows from stated facts |

**The trap in that example:** "more than 1000 books" *feels* true — libraries are big — but the
passage never says it. → Cannot Say.

## Tips

- **"All", "always", "never", "only"** are strong words. One counter-example makes them False.
- If a statement adds NEW information not in the passage → almost always **Cannot Say**.
- Don't argue with the passage — treat it as the only truth that exists.

# SECTION 3 — ABSTRACT / LOGICAL REASONING (shapes & patterns)

"Which shape comes next?" / "Which is the odd one out?" / "Complete the grid." No numbers or
words — just visual patterns. You look for **what is changing** from one shape to the next.

## What to check (run through these)

1. **Rotation** — is the shape turning? (90° each step? clockwise?)
2. **Count** — are dots/sides/lines increasing or decreasing? (1, 2, 3, …)
3. **Shading / colour** — flipping black↔white? filling in?
4. **Position** — is something moving around the box (corner to corner)?
5. **Size** — growing or shrinking?
6. **Adding / removing** — does a new element appear each step?

Often **two things change at once** (e.g. rotating AND gaining a dot). Track each separately.

## Worked example (described)

> A square with an arrow. Step 1: arrow points up. Step 2: points right. Step 3: points down.
> What's step 4?

The arrow rotates 90° clockwise each step: up → right → down → **left**. Answer: arrow left.

## Odd-one-out

Find the rule that **four of five share**, and the one that breaks it.
> Four shapes have an even number of sides (square, hexagon…), one has 5 (pentagon) → the
> pentagon is the odd one out.

Check: number of sides, shading, symmetry, number of shapes inside, orientation.

## Matrix (3×3 grid, one cell missing)

Read it like a table: look **across each row** for the rule, then **down each column**. The
missing cell must satisfy both.

# SECTION 4 — GENERAL MENTAL ABILITY / SPEED

Mixed quick questions and **string/number matching** (comparing two things for an exact match).

## Matching — the accuracy drill

> Are these identical? `48273901` vs `48279301`
> Compare in **chunks**: `4827` = `4827` ✓, then `3901` vs `9301` ✗ → **different**.

Tip: compare left-to-right in small groups. Most errors are transposed digits (`39` vs `93`).

## Quick logic

> "All roses are flowers. Some flowers fade quickly. Do all roses fade quickly?"
> **No / Cannot say** — "some flowers" isn't "all flowers", so you can't conclude roses do.
> (Same "use only what's stated" rule as verbal.)

# PRACTICE SET (do under a timer — ~45 seconds each)

**Sequences — find the next number:**
1. 3, 7, 11, 15, ?
2. 2, 4, 8, 16, ?
3. 1, 4, 9, 16, ?
4. 5, 10, 20, 40, ?
5. 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, ?
6. 100, 90, 81, 73, ?  (differences: −10, −9, −8, …)

**Percentages:**
7. 20% of 150 = ?
8. 25% of 80 = ?
9. 6% of 50 = ?  (use the swap trick)
10. What % is 15 of 60?

**Ratios & averages:**
11. Share 60 in ratio 4:1. What's the bigger share?
12. Average of 10, 20, 30, 40 = ?
13. Three numbers average 20. Two are 15 and 25. The third?

**Verbal** (passage: "All staff must wear a badge. Visitors sign in at reception. The office
closes at 6 pm."):
14. "Staff wear badges." → ?
15. "Visitors must wear badges." → ?
16. "The office is open at 5 pm." → ?
17. "There are 50 staff." → ?

**Logic:**
18. "Some cats are black. All black things absorb heat. Do some cats absorb heat?" → ?
19. Odd one out: circle, square, triangle, sphere, pentagon → ?
20. Matching — identical? `PQ7R3T` vs `PQ7R8T` → ?

---

## ANSWERS

1. **19** (+4) · 2. **32** (×2) · 3. **25** (squares, 5²) · 4. **80** (×2) · 5. **8** (Fibonacci)
· 6. **66** (−7 next: differences shrink by 1)
7. **30** (10%=15, ×2) · 8. **20** (quarter of 80) · 9. **3** (swap → 50% of 6) · 10. **25%** (15/60)
11. **48** (parts 4+1=5, one part 12, bigger = 4×12) · 12. **25** (100÷4) · 13. **20** (total 60 − 15 − 25)
14. **True** · 15. **Cannot Say** (only staff are stated to wear badges) · 16. **True** (closes at 6, so open at 5) · 17. **Cannot Say** (number not given)
18. **Yes** (some cats are black → those black cats absorb heat → some cats do) · 19. **sphere** (the only 3-D shape) · 20. **Different** (`R3T` vs `R8T`)

## The night-before / morning checklist

- Do 2–3 timed practice sets in the days before (search "free numerical reasoning test",
  "free abstract reasoning test" — 123test.com, AssessmentDay, Practice Aptitude Tests).
- **Sleep well** — cognitive scores drop measurably when tired.
- **Speed rule:** 30–60 seconds per question. Stuck? Guess and move on (no penalty = never blank).
- **Accuracy over completion** — these are designed so most people don't finish.
- Keep scratch paper for the numerical section — don't do it all in your head.
- Verbal: **only the passage**. Logic: **only what's stated**. Ignore real-world knowledge.
